# Marking criteria

Level 6 criteria. Assessed against the requirements and these four criteria. Assesses learning outcomes 1, 2, 3 and 4. The overall grade is set using the School of Business & Technology Assessment Criteria Grid.

## Knowledge & Understanding

- **80+%:** Extraordinary and authoritative understanding of the five selected software errors, including root causes, exploit mechanisms, secure mitigations, verification, the Security Development Life Cycle and product risk.
- **70–79%:** Excellent and wide-ranging understanding of the five software errors, their causes, exploitation, mitigation and retesting, with confident understanding of secure development lifecycle and risk principles.
- **60–69%:** Very good understanding of the selected vulnerabilities, exploitation, mitigation and verification. Secure development lifecycle and risk concepts are accurately explained, with only minor gaps or inaccuracies.
- **50–59%:** Sound understanding of the main vulnerabilities and their mitigations. Most exploitation and retesting processes are explained correctly, although some technical, lifecycle or risk concepts lack depth or precision.
- **40–49%:** Basic but sufficient understanding of the five software errors. Core causes, attacks and mitigations are identified, but explanations contain omissions or inaccuracies and lifecycle, risk or verification knowledge is limited.
- **30–39%:** Limited and partly inaccurate understanding, with major omissions across the five errors, exploitation, mitigation or retesting. Secure development lifecycle and product-risk discussion is superficial or mechanically reproduced.
- **20–29%:** Very limited subject knowledge with many errors and omissions. Few assessment elements are meaningfully completed, and the relationships between vulnerability, exploitation, mitigation and verification are poorly understood.
- **10–19%:** Seriously flawed knowledge of secure coding. The submission shows little accurate understanding of the selected vulnerabilities or of how they are exploited, tested, mitigated and retested.
- **1–9%:** No meaningful secure-coding knowledge or practical understanding is demonstrated.

## Analysis

- **80+%:** Exceptional critical analysis of vulnerable and mitigated code. Exploitation, appropriate tool-assisted testing and retesting are interpreted rigorously; security decisions, limitations and residual risks are evaluated convincingly.
- **70–79%:** Excellent and consistent analysis of practical evidence. Vulnerable and mitigated code, exploits, security-tool findings and retests are interpreted critically, with well-justified controls and thoughtful consideration of limitations and residual risk.
- **60–69%:** Very good analysis of code and testing evidence, mainly consistent throughout. Appropriate tools and retesting support the conclusions, although evaluation of alternatives, limitations or residual risks may lack some depth.
- **50–59%:** Sound analysis with some interpretation of vulnerable code, exploits, tool findings and before-and-after testing. Decisions are generally reasonable but justification, critical comparison or evaluation of tool limitations is uneven.
- **40–49%:** Basic analysis and collation of practical evidence. Some testing and retesting are demonstrated, but interpretation is mainly descriptive, tool use is limited or uncritical, and mitigation claims are not always convincingly verified.
- **30–39%:** Predominantly descriptive work with limited or unreliable testing. Tool outputs or screenshots receive little interpretation; mitigations and retesting are weak or incomplete, and important conclusions lack verifiable practical evidence.
- **20–29%:** Very limited attempt to analyse the task. Practical demonstrations are incomplete, invalid or unsupported; little meaningful testing, tool interpretation, mitigation evaluation or retesting is evident.
- **10–19%:** Seriously flawed attempt at analysis that does not meaningfully address the task. There is no credible interpretation of code, testing evidence, security-tool findings, mitigations or risk.
- **1–9%:** No meaningful attempt is made to analyse or address the practical and critical requirements of the assessment.

## Reading and Research

- **80+%:** Extensive, independent and critical research. Authoritative standards and high-quality academic or technical sources are integrated throughout to substantiate technical claims, mitigations, lifecycle discussion and risk analysis.
- **70–79%:** Excellent independent research using a wide range of relevant academic and authoritative technical sources. Evidence is selected critically, integrated effectively and used consistently to support claims.
- **60–69%:** Very good independent research using a good range of relevant academic and authoritative technical sources. Most technical claims are supported and sources are generally integrated effectively.
- **50–59%:** Some independent research using an appropriate range of mostly relevant sources. Key concepts are supported, although source quality, breadth, currency or integration is inconsistent.
- **40–49%:** Basic independent research using a limited range of relevant sources. Some claims are supported, but important technical assertions are uncited and source quality or referencing accuracy is variable.
- **30–39%:** Limited research with over-reliance on generic websites, lecture material or a narrow source base. Strong assertions are frequently unsupported and sources are poorly selected, integrated or referenced.
- **20–29%:** Little credible research. Discussion relies mainly on personal opinion, unreliable or inappropriate sources, with few supported technical claims and seriously deficient citation practice.
- **10–19%:** It is unclear whether meaningful reading has taken place. Credible sources are absent or not cited, and technical claims are largely unsupported or materially inaccurate.
- **1–9%:** No credible reading or research is apparent or cited.

## Writing/Presentation Style

- **80+%:** Lively, precise and professional report with exceptional control of argument. Code, screenshots and tool results are clearly captioned, referenced and explained; appendices are complete; virtually error-free.
- **70–79%:** Excellent, coherent and professional presentation with strong control of argument. Figures, code and test evidence are consistently captioned, referenced and discussed; source-code appendices are complete; very few errors.
- **60–69%:** Very good, logical and professional report with few language or formatting errors. Code, screenshots and results are generally well captioned, referenced and explained, with an appropriate appendix.
- **50–59%:** Sound and generally coherent presentation with some language or formatting errors. Most practical evidence is identifiable and discussed, but captioning, cross-referencing, labelling or appendix organisation is inconsistent.
- **40–49%:** Broadly coherent report with noticeable language and presentation errors. Code and screenshots are included, but some are poorly captioned, insufficiently explained or disconnected from the narrative; appendices may be incomplete.
- **30–39%:** Limited structure and coherence. Vulnerable and mitigated versions are difficult to distinguish; code or screenshots are often uncaptioned or unreferenced; appendix, formatting or required referencing conventions are inadequately followed.
- **20–29%:** Little structure or coherence. Practical evidence is difficult to follow, important code or results are missing or unexplained, and frequent language and formatting problems substantially hinder understanding.
- **10–19%:** Much of the submission cannot be understood because of seriously flawed structure, language or presentation. Required practical evidence, source code or report components are largely absent or unusable.
- **1–9%:** The submission is fragmentary, illegible or does not follow the required assessment format.

- **0%:** Assessment not submitted.
